Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about what this campaign is asking for, what it would mean for residents, and what happens next.

What is this campaign actually asking for?

For Acres Homes to become its own independent city — with its own elected city council, its own budget, and direct local control over services like streets, drainage, parks, and public safety — instead of being one part of the much larger City of Houston.

Is Acres Homes currently part of the City of Houston?

Acres Homes was historically an independent, unincorporated community and was later annexed into the City of Houston. (Verify exact annexation date and current legal boundary status before publishing.) Because of that history, incorporation for Acres Homes likely requires first being released or detached from Houston — see The Process for details.

Will my property taxes go up?

It depends on the services the new city chooses to provide directly versus contract out, and on tax rates set by an elected local council once formed. Many small, newly incorporated or enclaved cities keep rates modest by contracting for services like fire response rather than building full departments immediately. This campaign will publish a plain-language cost analysis as it becomes available — no specific tax rate has been set or promised.

What happens to my Houston services (water, HPD, HFD, trash pickup)?

This depends entirely on the transition agreement negotiated as part of any detachment/incorporation process, and on choices the new city council would make. Many incorporated Texas cities contract with neighboring utilities or county agencies for water, fire, or dispatch services rather than building them from scratch. This is a major open question the committee is researching and will detail here as answers firm up.

Does the City of Houston have to agree to this?

Under at least one possible path (a negotiated release), yes. Under others (a statutory disannexation petition, if applicable, or special state legislation), Houston's agreement may not be legally required, though it would still likely be politically contested. See The Process for the full breakdown, and note this section should be confirmed by an attorney.

What about Acres Homes ISD / schools?

Public school district boundaries are generally separate from municipal boundaries in Texas. Acres Homes schools are part of a school district (verify: confirm current ISD) and incorporation as a city would not, by itself, change school district lines.

How long could this take?

Realistically, years rather than months — this involves legal research, sustained organizing, negotiation or legislative action, and likely a public vote. The timeline depends heavily on which legal path turns out to be viable.

How can I help right now?

Sign the petition, share your own Acres Homes story, and volunteer your time or professional expertise (legal, government, communications, organizing). Visit Get Involved to do all three.
