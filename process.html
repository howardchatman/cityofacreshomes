Legal & Civic Path

How this actually happens

Incorporation is a legal process governed by the Texas Local Government Code — and Acres Homes' situation has one important wrinkle worth understanding before we organize around it.

!This is not legal advice

This page is a plain-language starting point for organizing, written from general knowledge of Texas municipal law. It is not a substitute for advice from a licensed Texas attorney who handles municipal/local government law, and it has not been reviewed by one. Before circulating this page publicly or taking formal legal steps, the committee should have it checked by counsel and by staff from your Texas House and Senate offices.

Background

How incorporation normally works in Texas

For land that is unincorporated (not already part of any city), Texas law provides a fairly well-worn path:

  • Step 1

    Confirm eligibility

    Under the Local Government Code, a community generally needs a minimum population and must not already sit inside another city's limits or, in some cases, its extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ) without that city's consent.

  • Step 2

    Petition of residents

    A petition signed by a qualifying number of registered voters within the proposed city boundary is filed with the county.

  • Step 3

    Election called

    An election is held for voters within the proposed boundary to approve or reject incorporation.

  • Step 4

    New city organizes

    If approved, the new city elects officers and begins operating, typically first as a "general law" city (Type A, B, or C depending on population), with the option to later adopt a home-rule charter once it passes a population threshold.

The Wrinkle

Acres Homes is already inside Houston's city limits

This is the single most important fact to organize around. The standard "incorporate unincorporated land" process above applies to areas outside any city. Acres Homes, by contrast, was annexed into the City of Houston decades ago and is part of Houston's corporate boundary today (verify current status and exact annexation history against Harris County and City of Houston records). That means becoming an independent city isn't a simple incorporation — it most likely requires Acres Homes to first be released, or detached, from Houston.

AService-failure disannexation

Texas law (Local Government Code, Chapter 43) has historically allowed residents of a recently annexed area to petition for disannexation if the annexing city failed to provide the services promised in its annexation service plan within a set period. This route is generally time-limited to a window after annexation and may not be available for areas annexed long ago — a key question for an attorney to confirm for Acres Homes specifically.

BNegotiated release

The City of Houston could, in theory, agree through its own council action to release Acres Homes' boundary back to unincorporated status, clearing the way for a standard incorporation election. This is a political outcome, not an automatic legal right — it requires building enough public and Council pressure to make it happen.

CSpecial legislation

The Texas Legislature can pass a special law addressing a specific city's boundary or a specific community's path to incorporation. This route runs through your Texas House and Senate members and typically requires strong, organized, documented local support to move forward — exactly what this campaign is building.

Our Plan

What the campaign is organizing to do

  • Now

    Build documented resident support

    Collect signatures, stories, and turnout at community meetings to demonstrate this is a broad, organized resident priority — not a handful of individuals.

  • Next

    Get a formal legal opinion

    Retain or secure pro bono counsel experienced in Texas municipal law to confirm which legal path (A, B, or C above) is realistically available to Acres Homes today.

  • Then

    Engage elected officials

    Bring the documented support and legal opinion to Houston City Council members, Harris County officials, and Acres Homes' Texas House and Senate representatives.

  • Then

    File & campaign for the applicable process

    Whether that's a disannexation petition, a negotiated release, or a legislative bill, run a public campaign to see it through — and, if it reaches that stage, an incorporation election.

Have legal or legislative experience? We need you.

This campaign is actively looking for attorneys, former city officials, and anyone with experience in Texas municipal law or the Legislature to help confirm the right path forward.
