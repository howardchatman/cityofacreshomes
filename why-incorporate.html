The Case for Incorporation

Other communities around Houston govern themselves. Acres Homes can too.

Incorporation isn't a radical idea — it's a proven, common structure across Greater Houston. Several communities today are independent, self-governing cities even though the City of Houston surrounds them on every side.

What changes

What incorporation would mean for Acres Homes

1A city council of our own

Elected officials whose entire job is representing Acres Homes — not one of 11 council districts inside a city of over two million residents.

2Local budget priorities

Tax revenue generated within our boundaries funds decisions made by our own council: street repair, drainage, parks, code enforcement, and public safety contracts, set by local priority rather than citywide formula.

3Zoning & land-use say

An independent city can adopt its own land-use and development rules — protecting the large-lot, multi-generational character residents value.

4Direct accountability

Residents can vote out their entire city government in a local election — a much shorter, clearer line of accountability than one seat on a large city council.

5Contracted services, kept local

Many small Texas cities contract for services (fire, dispatch, water) while keeping budget and policy control local — incorporation doesn't require building every department from scratch.

6Identity & permanence

A recognized city boundary protects Acres Homes' name, history, and borders from being redrawn or deprioritized in someone else's master plan.

Being Honest

Tradeoffs worth weighing, not hiding

A credible campaign names the costs too. These are the questions residents should expect — and that the eventual legal/financial feasibility study (see The Process) should answer with real numbers.

$Standing up a government costs money

Even a small city needs administration, insurance, elections, and at minimum contracted services. Early years often mean a leaner service level while a tax base and staff are built up.

🔒Loss of big-city scale

Houston's size buys economies of scale — large capital projects, bulk purchasing, and a big permanent workforce — that a new small city won't have on day one, though many contract around this (see card 5 above).

⚖Legal uncertainty and timeline

As The Process page explains, Acres Homes' path isn't a simple incorporation election — it likely requires detachment from Houston first, which could take years and is not guaranteed to succeed.

?Services during any transition

Any plan needs a clear answer for how police, fire, water, and drainage keep functioning without interruption during and immediately after a change in jurisdiction.
